## Today's Posture

Defense and patience — trim into strength, do not chase. Hold the core position, keep the trim ladder live into any push back toward the 7,780–7,794 zone, and make no adds at spot. This is the sixth consecutive session pinned beneath an unconfirmed record high, and the character of the tape has shifted from consolidation to something that increasingly resembles distribution.

Three things justify the defensive tilt today. Price closed lower for a third straight session and slipped marginally below a flattening 20-day moving average for the first time in this advance — a short-term regime test, not a routine dip. Money flow has decayed hard: the five-day money-flow reading has fallen from the low-90s on August 4 to the mid-40s today at a broadly comparable price, and daily RSI has faded from the mid-60s to the high-40s. And the valuation cushion is effectively gone, with the equity risk premium pinned inside the ceiling band.

None of this is a sell signal. Credit is the tightest it has been all year, volatility is subdued and below its 50-day average, and the 50-day sits well above a rising 200-day. The trend is intact; the *marginal* opportunity is not. Zero of five buy conditions are satisfied against three of five trim conditions, so patience is the position.

The equity risk premium is stable at a compressed level, which the classification treats as a neutral-to-weakening structural backdrop: there is no deterioration in trend, but there is also no cushion being rebuilt. Credit is the one unambiguously supportive input — the junk-versus-investment-grade spread has tightened to a year-low shelf, and the spread chart shows a clean multi-week downtrend into that level with no hint of the spikes that marked prior risk episodes. Credit is not warning.

Narrow leadership at record price, combined with an elevated-but-cooling extension and a capped valuation, is the definition of the late-bull regime. The threshold bar stays at 70%, and the 23.6% retracement remains a hard pause zone with no deployment permitted.

## Evidence and Tensions

Five tensions remain open, and today's posture resolves each toward patience rather than either extreme.

**Breadth improvement versus narrow leadership at record price.** The bullish read is that the McClellan Volume Summation Index has recovered to just under 1,000 from a cycle low near 830 — the strongest breadth in roughly two months, suggesting participation is quietly rebuilding beneath a flat index. The bearish read is that net new 52-week highs have decayed to barely above the flatline while the index trades within 1% of an all-time high, the narrowest leadership of the advance, and McClellan remains well below its February and April peaks despite record price. Resolution: breadth improvement is acknowledged but not yet confirming. Narrow leadership at highs elevates caution, and it is one of the three trim conditions met today. It does not create a sell signal because credit is not corroborating the warning.

**Money-flow divergence versus a normal pause.** Bullishly, daily money flow on the one-month and three-month charts still holds in the mid-60s with RSI in neutral territory — entirely consistent with a healthy consolidation. Bearishly, the five-day reading has collapsed from above 90 on August 4 to the mid-40s today at a comparable price, with daily RSI fading from the mid-60s to the high-40s — a multi-session bearish divergence indicating the bid is withdrawing under a flat tape. Resolution: the divergence is real and carries high weight, which is why no adds are permitted at spot; but because the longer timeframes remain neutral rather than broken, the response is trim bias, not risk reduction.

**Valuation ceiling versus intact trend.** Bullishly, rising forward EPS and a forward multiple only modestly rich argue for holding an intact uptrend with tightening credit. Bearishly, the ERP inside the 0.0%–0.5% band leaves no cushion, and the ERP-confirmed floor sits about 4% below spot. Resolution: hold the core, deploy nothing new until price reaches the zone where valuation and the deep fib cluster align.

**The 20-day breach versus credit calm.** Bullishly, a subdued VIX below its 50-day and year-low credit spreads say this is orderly profit-taking with the rising 50-day as a robust backstop. Bearishly, a close below a flattening 20-day after an extended advance is a regime shift, and it arrives with a third consecutive lower close inside a six-session shelf. Resolution: treated as a short-term regime test that raises the bar for bullish action without triggering defense beyond trimming.

**Greed sentiment without internal confirmation.** Bullishly, Fear & Greed in the low 60s reflects restored risk appetite far short of February's euphoria. Bearishly, greed at record price with net new highs at the flatline and momentum rolling over is textbook narrow-leadership topping behavior, and the index has already ticked down from its recent peak. Resolution: sentiment is neutral-to-cautionary, adding weight to the trim bias without independently forcing action.

Confirming evidence remains substantial and is the reason this is a defensive hold rather than a reduction: an unbroken higher-high structure, credit at year-low spreads, subdued volatility, price above every retracement level, and forward earnings still rising. The governing tension is that the best credit and volatility backdrop of the entire arc is colliding with a six-session failure to extend beneath an unconfirmed record high — and with the probability edge back to a coin flip, the disciplined answer is to wait.
