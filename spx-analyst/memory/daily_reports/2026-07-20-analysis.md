Until then, the tactical stance is straightforward: protect capital, hold existing exposure, and monitor the lower fib and liquidation levels for a genuine setup.

## Market Regime

The structural bias remains Late Bull / Topping, and today's action is that regime doing exactly what it does: grinding at resistance, failing, and beginning a corrective leg while the internals refuse to confirm. The classification rests on four checks, and each one reinforces caution beneath an intact long-term trend.

Price extension is moderate — the index sits about 6.5% above its 200-day moving average, comfortably inside the normal-to-moderate band and nowhere near the extreme readings that precede violent mean reversion. That is the primary support for treating this as a maturing bull rather than a broken one.

The valuation backdrop, however, has deteriorated to the ceiling. The equity risk premium is effectively zero and stable — no expansion to provide structural support, and no cushion for aggressive dip-buying. Credit is not confirming either: junk spreads sit above their recent average and have refused to tighten alongside the price recovery, a quiet divergence that has persisted for weeks.

Breadth is the clearest warning. The McClellan Volume Summation Index sits near its lows around the 880 level, and net new 52-week highs have faded to roughly 2.0 — a narrow, deteriorating participation profile that never validated the run back toward the swing high. When breadth and credit both diverge from price during a topping process, caution earns more weight than a rising index.

Put together: healthy long-term trend, extended-but-not-extreme price, but weakening valuation support, non-confirming credit, and narrow breadth. That is a textbook Late Bull / Topping configuration, and it justifies the 70% action threshold and the defensive lean.
