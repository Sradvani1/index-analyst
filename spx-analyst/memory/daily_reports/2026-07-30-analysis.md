## Market Regime

The structural bias remains Late Bull / Topping, unchanged for a seventh consecutive session. Four regime checks explain why the label survives a strong up day.

Extension is benign. Price sits roughly 6% above a steadily rising 200-day average, squarely in the moderate-extension band, which is one of the few genuinely healthy features of the tape. This is not a stretched market being pulled back by gravity; the 200-day has been climbing all year and the longer-horizon charts show a clean, uninterrupted advance off the March lows with price still far above the 125-day.

Valuation support is the binding constraint. The equity risk premium is stable but sits inside the zone the framework treats as a valuation ceiling — a cushion thin enough that any earnings or rate shock has nothing to absorb it. Stable is not expanding, so structural support is not improving; it is simply no longer deteriorating.

Credit is the quiet positive. The junk-versus-investment-grade spread sits at or just below its one-year average and well off the June spike, so debt markets are not flashing the warning that would normally accompany a topping process.

Breadth is the disqualifier. McClellan volume summation has ground down to its cycle-low reference in a persistent downtrend since May, and NYSE net new highs have faded to a weak positive reading. Falling participation while the index rallies is textbook narrow leadership. With extension moderate and credit neutral but valuation capped and breadth contracting, the market qualifies as a maturing bull working through a topping process — which raises the bar for bullish action to 70% and turns the shallow retracement band into a pause zone rather than a deployment level.
