## Market Regime

The structural bias remains Late Bull / Topping, unchanged for a seventh consecutive session. The defining feature of this regime is the collision between a mechanically healthy trend and internals that will not validate the price recovery — a signature late-cycle configuration.

The trend backbone is genuinely intact. The 50-day sits well above a rising 200-day, price holds roughly +7.78% above the 200-day (moderate extension, not extreme), and the 125-day moving average sits far below spot. Volatility is calm, with the VIX in the mid-16s and beneath its own 50-day average. On price and trend structure alone, this reads like a functioning bull market.

But the other three regime pillars diverge. Equity risk premium is pinned near zero, deep in the valuation-ceiling band, offering no fundamental support for aggressive adds. Credit is not confirming — junk spreads have widened to the upper end of their recent range, an Extreme Fear reading. And breadth is the loudest dissenter: the McClellan Summation Index sits near the weak end of its range around 893, net new 52-week highs are a thin 1.85%, and safe-haven demand shows bonds modestly outperforming stocks over the trailing 20 days.

Three of the four master-context checks — valuation, credit, breadth — argue caution, while only the price/trend pillar argues strength. That asymmetry is precisely why the regime stays Late Bull / Topping and why the action bar for bullish deployment is set high. In this environment, rallies into resistance are to be sold or trimmed, not chased, until participation broadens.

## Evidence and Tensions

The intact bull skeleton is real, but it stands largely alone against a wall of non-confirmation — and that asymmetry is what anchors the trim-and-hold posture. The confirming case is genuine: the 50-day rides above a rising 200-day with price +7.78% over the long-term average, the VIX is benign and below its 50-day average keeping moving-average support usable, price has recovered above the 20-day and the deeper fibs, and no liquidation thresholds are in play. Under normal conditions, that would support a constructive stance.

Three tensions override it:

- **Breadth and credit versus the rally (high weight).** The bullish read is that price has held its multi-week recovery above the 50-day and reclaimed the 20-day, implying the pullback is over. The bearish read is that the McClellan index sits near the weak end of its range, net new highs are a thin 1.85%, safe-haven demand favors bonds, and junk spreads have widened to 1.46% — internals refusing to confirm. Today's posture resolves this in favor of caution: when both breadth and credit diverge from price, the advance is treated as narrow leadership into resistance, not a durable breakout.

- **Valuation ceiling versus the trend (high weight).** Bulls point to the intact trend above key averages; the counter is that ERP is pinned near zero with forward PE around 20x and the 10-year at 4.49%, leaving no cushion for adds. Resolution: valuation vetoes aggressive deployment even as the trend holds — trim bias, no adds.

- **Stalled rally versus the skeleton (medium weight).** The optimistic read is a near breakout within 1.3% of the swing high; the sober read is two rejections at 7,498, back-to-back weak closes, and a down-first probability model failing every threshold. Resolution: signals are actionable only when they align — and here they do not, so patience wins.

The synthesis is a market whose long-term trend is healthy but whose immediate advance is unconfirmed, unsupported by valuation, and modeled to slip lower first. Trim into strength, hold, and wait for price and participation to confirm before re-engaging.
