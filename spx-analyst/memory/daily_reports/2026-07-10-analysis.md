## Today's Posture

The posture for the next session is to trim and defend at resistance — scale trims into strength as price tags the June swing high, hold the core, and make no aggressive adds. This is a disciplined defensive tilt, not a bearish call: the underlying trend remains intact and momentum has clearly improved, but the market is grinding into hard resistance with a valuation cushion that has thinned to nearly zero.

The reason for holding the trim bias despite a strong tape is the collision at the top of the range. Price has recovered to within a whisker of the 6/15 swing high on the strongest up-first probability skew of the entire recovery, and volatility has collapsed to a complacent floor. That is genuine strength. But it is arriving with breadth near its cycle lows, credit refusing to endorse the highs, and an equity risk premium pinned in the ceiling band — the classic late-cycle signature where price outruns its internals.

The signal balance reflects this: trim signals outweigh buy signals three to one. The right move is to let strength do the work of raising exposure into resistance, harvesting into the swing-high test rather than chasing a breakout that internals have not yet ratified.

## Market Regime

The structural regime remains Late Bull / Topping, and today's evidence reinforces rather than challenges that classification. The bull skeleton is mechanically intact — the 50-day sits well above a rising 200-day, and price is roughly 8.8% above the 200-day, which is a moderate rather than extreme extension. That moderate extension is why the drift input remains constructive and the rally exhaustion score reads Low, with no discount applied to upside probabilities. On the trend mechanics alone, this looks like a healthy bull.

What keeps the regime in the topping bucket is the growing gap between price and its supporting evidence across the other layers:

- **Breadth is not confirming.** The McClellan Volume Summation Index sits near its cycle lows around 941, and net new 52-week highs read a muted strength of roughly 2.14 — both well off the readings seen during earlier legs of this advance. Price is making new recovery highs while participation stays narrow.
- **Credit is not endorsing.** The junk bond yield spread hovers right around its recent average near 1.30 and has repeatedly spiked rather than tightening into the rally. Credit is not warning loudly, but it is conspicuously declining to confirm.
- **Valuation support has weakened.** The equity risk premium has slipped to 0.37%, deep in the valuation-ceiling band, as the 10-year holds near 4.57%. There is essentially no cushion left.

The equity risk premium trend is stable rather than expanding, so the valuation backdrop is neutral-to-restrictive rather than deteriorating outright. Taken together, an intact trend paired with narrow breadth, non-confirming credit, and a zero-cushion valuation is the textbook late-cycle setup — momentum can persist, but the bar for aggressive bullish action stays high.

## Evidence and Tensions

The defining tension is an intact, momentum-improving bull skeleton colliding with late-cycle confirmation risk at resistance. Price is at the swing high on a 78% up-first edge with a complacent sub-16 VIX, while the equity risk premium is pinned at the valuation ceiling, breadth sits near its cycle lows, and credit refuses to endorse the highs. Today's posture resolves this by trimming into strength — respecting the trend and probability edge without chasing a rally the internals have not ratified.

**Breadth and credit diverging from price.** The bullish read is that strong momentum can drag breadth higher as a breakout confirms itself. The bearish read is that the McClellan index near cycle lows and muted net new 52-week highs signal narrow leadership carrying the index to new recovery highs — a classic topping divergence. Resolution: this high-weight divergence is the primary reason conviction is capped; trims into strength are favored until breadth turns up to validate the price recovery.

**Valuation ceiling versus the bull trend.** The bullish read is that trend structure is intact and the probability edge favors an upside-first path. The bearish read is that a 0.37% ERP with the 10-year at 4.57% and forward P/E at 20.26x leaves zero cushion, arguing for trim bias and no aggressive adds. Resolution: with valuation restrictive at the ceiling, the trend justifies holding the core but not deploying fresh capital at resistance.

**Credit not confirming.** The bullish read is that the junk spread near 1.30 sits at its recent average, not widening dramatically. The bearish read is that the spread remains elevated versus early-year lows and has repeatedly spiked rather than tightening into the equity recovery. Resolution: credit is treated as a non-confirming caution flag that reinforces, but does not by itself drive, the defensive tilt.

Confirming evidence stands on the other side of the ledger and is real: a rising moving-average stack, price at the highs of the recovery, a benign VIX floor, recovered neutral sentiment, non-complacent put/call, and a probability edge clearing all thresholds. That evidence is why the posture is a measured trim into strength rather than an outright defensive reduction — the trend earns the benefit of the doubt, but the internals earn the discipline.
