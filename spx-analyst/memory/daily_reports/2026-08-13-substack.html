SPX Breaks Higher, but the Late-Cycle Caution Remains

A record close clears the seven-session shelf, while narrow breadth, elevated extension, and a thin equity risk premium argue for patience rather than fresh buying.

The Takeaway

The S&P 500 closed at a record 7,798.99, up 0.65%, and finally moved above the seven-session 7,700 to 7,794 congestion shelf. The close came in the top third of the daily range, a meaningful improvement from the weaker closes seen earlier in August.

This is a genuine bullish development, but it does not remove the market’s late-cycle risks. The index is now 10.31% above its rising 200-day average and is riding the expanding upper Bollinger Band. Breadth remains narrow, the equity risk premium is only 0.29%, and short-term money flow has not confirmed the new high.

The practical posture remains a trim bias. Respect the breakout by avoiding premature short positions, but do not chase the record. The preferred approach is to hold a core exposure, lighten incrementally into the 7,817 to 7,915 area, and wait for better valuation and technical support before considering new deployment.

What Happened Today

Today’s advance cleared both the prior shelf and the previous intraday record near 7,793.68. The index briefly reached about 7,816.70, then gave back much of the early move before recovering through the afternoon and closing near the highs. That recovery showed buyers remained in control despite substantial intraday movement.

The short-term structure also improved. SPX reclaimed the rising 20-day average near 7,760 after slipping below it on August 11. The 50-day average sits at 7,507.75 and remains well above the rising 200-day average at 7,070.35. That alignment continues to define a healthy long-term uptrend.

Momentum is constructive but not extreme. Daily RSI is about 66 and daily MFI is about 69. Both readings support the advance, though neither has reached the more stretched levels that previously marked a clear trim zone. Credit markets are also supportive, with the junk bond spread over investment-grade debt at 1.22, its one-year low.

The less encouraging change is that participation weakened again. Net new 52-week highs fell to 0.92, the weakest reading of the year, while the McClellan measure recovered only to about 1,030. The index is making records, but fewer stocks are participating than during earlier parts of the advance.

Why It Matters

The breakout matters because the market had spent seven sessions pressing against the same upper boundary. A close above that shelf can signal that the prior consolidation has resolved upward and that the next leg is beginning. The strong closing location adds credibility compared with a session that finishes near its lows.

However, the quality of a breakout depends on more than price. The current advance still has the profile of a mature trend. SPX is well above its major moving averages, and the expanding upper Bollinger Band shows strong momentum alongside increasing extension. The index is not breaking out from a low-risk base near long-term support. It is breaking out after a substantial run.

Valuation adds another constraint. The forward price-to-earnings ratio is 20.28, a level where buying requires strong supporting conditions. Forward earnings have continued to rise, reaching 384.63, so the advance is not simply being driven by a static earnings base. Still, the equity risk premium of 0.29% offers little cushion over Treasury yields. That supports patience even if the trend remains durable.

Sentiment is calm rather than fearful. Fear and Greed is 66, the VIX is 14.63, and the VIX remains well below its roughly 17.2 50-day average. These readings are consistent with an orderly risk-on market, but they also mean there is no fear-driven washout providing an obvious high-quality entry point.

Levels and Signals to Watch

The first upside area is 7,817 to 7,915. This is the projected drift and acceleration band, and it is also the zone where incremental trimming is favored if strength continues.

The new intraday high near 7,816.70 remains provisional. A second qualifying close is needed to confirm the new anchor. If the breakout fails, the first test would likely be the 7,698 area. That level is the 23.6% retracement and the upper edge of the former shelf. It is a pause and monitoring zone, not the preferred re-entry level.

The primary re-entry area is near 7,625, the 38.2% retracement. The secondary level is around 7,565, near the 50% retracement. The strongest structural confluence sits near 7,506 to 7,508, where the 61.8% retracement meets the rising 50-day average. The valuation-based re-entry floor is near 7,481.62, which is roughly 4.1% below spot.

The key confirmation is breadth improvement. A record close would be more convincing if net new highs and the McClellan measure began expanding rather than merely recovering from weak readings. A failed move back into the 7,700 to 7,794 shelf would weaken the breakout case. A move below the support hierarchy would increase the odds of a broader mean-reversion phase.

The Bull Case

The bullish case begins with price. SPX broke through a well-defined shelf and closed near its high, while the 20-day average was reclaimed. The 50-day average remains comfortably above the 200-day average, confirming that the larger trend is still positive.

Credit is the strongest supporting signal. Narrowing junk bond spreads indicate that risk appetite remains healthy outside equities as well. Forward earnings are also moving higher, which gives the market a fundamental foundation and reduces the risk that the entire advance is based only on expanding valuation multiples.

Breadth could still repair itself after the index leads. The McClellan measure has improved from its cycle low, and daily momentum remains bullish without reaching the most extreme RSI and MFI readings. If participation broadens and the new high near 7,816.70 receives confirmation, continuation toward the upper 7,817 to 7,915 band becomes the more credible path.

The Risk Case

The risk is that price has broken upward without enough participation. Net new 52-week highs are at their weakest level of the year, and the current McClellan recovery remains well below the stronger readings seen during earlier advances. A record index supported by a shrinking group of stocks can continue higher, but it is more vulnerable to a sharp reversal.

Extension and valuation reinforce that concern. SPX is more than 10% above its 200-day average, the upper Bollinger Band is expanding, and the equity risk premium is pinned inside a valuation ceiling. There is little room for aggressive multiple expansion unless earnings upgrades continue to improve the market’s cushion.

Short-term money flow also trails price. Five-day MFI is near 49, far below its reading near 91 on August 4 when the index was lower. That unresolved divergence suggests the latest high has not attracted the same level of buying pressure. With the VIX near a one-year low, any pullback may be orderly rather than capitulative, offering less evidence of a durable bottom.

Bottom Line

The breakout deserves respect, but it does not yet justify chasing. Price, credit, and the broader trend are bullish, while breadth, valuation, extension, and money flow remain cautionary. That combination keeps the market in a late-bull phase with a trim tilt rather than a fresh accumulation phase.

The preferred plan is to keep a core position, scale out gradually into 7,817 to 7,915, and avoid adding new capital while breadth remains narrow and the equity risk premium stays near 0.29%. A pullback toward 7,698 should be monitored, but stronger re-entry work belongs closer to 7,625 or the 7,506 to 7,508 confluence near the 50-day average. The next important test is whether the new high confirms and whether participation finally expands to support it.
