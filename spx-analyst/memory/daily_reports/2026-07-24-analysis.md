## Today's Posture

Yesterday's 1.2% break has not been repaired, and today's flat close leaves the index below the 20-day, below the flattening 50-day, and below the entire 23.6%–61.8% retracement band of the July 8–15 leg. The case for waiting is that four of five trim/defense conditions are satisfied while only one buy condition is met. Breadth sits near cycle lows, the equity risk premium is effectively zero against a 10-year that pushed higher again, volatility has climbed back above its 50-day average, and the near-term probability skew is decisively down-first, failing every regime threshold for a second straight session. Against that, the intraday tape did stabilize — a mid-afternoon slide into the session low was bought back into a strong close in the upper third of the range, and five-day money flow turned up off its trough.

That single constructive tell is not enough. Re-entry requires at least three of four confirmations; today delivers one to two at best, because volatility is rising rather than reversing and participation is still contracting. Keep dry powder, respect the caution shelf below, and let the market earn the next allocation.

## Market Regime

The structural bias remains Late Bull / Topping, unchanged through a seventh consecutive session, and today's evidence hardens rather than softens that classification. The four regime inputs line up as follows.

- **Extension:** price sits roughly 5.8% above the 200-day, the moderate-extension band. This is the least stretched reading of the recent run and is the main reason the long-term skeleton still qualifies as a bull structure rather than an outright break.
- **Equity risk premium:** stable, but stable at the floor. The cushion over risk-free yield is functionally absent, which places valuation in the ceiling band where trim bias governs and aggressive adds are disallowed regardless of price level.
- **Credit:** neutral. Junk-versus-investment-grade spreads have eased back from the 1.35–1.38 area to sit essentially on their one-year average, and the one-year spread history shows nothing resembling the November 2025 or March 2026 spikes. Credit is not confirming a risk-off break.
- **Breadth:** deteriorating and the weakest link. The McClellan Volume Summation Index has ground down through a series of lower highs since the May peak and now sits in cycle-low territory, while net new 52-week highs on the NYSE have faded from the early-May surge back toward the flat line.

The combination — moderate extension, zero valuation cushion, neutral credit, and contracting participation while the index sits only about 2.2% off its swing high — is the textbook late-cycle signature: narrow leadership carrying an index that no longer has valuation support beneath it. Late Bull / Topping carries the 70% action threshold and treats the entire 23.6% retracement region as a hard pause zone, not a deployment level. Both constraints bind today.

## Valuation and ERP

Valuation is the structural constraint on any bullish impulse, and it has not improved. The forward multiple sits in the moderate-buying band on its own terms, and the forward earnings yield is the best of this recent run — but the comparison that matters is against the risk-free alternative, and there the 10-year's push higher has kept the equity risk premium pinned at the very bottom of the valuation-ceiling range.

Practically, that means equities are offering essentially no compensation over Treasuries. The posture guide is unambiguous at this level: trim bias, no aggressive adds, irrespective of how attractive a fib retracement looks on the chart. It also means the valuation-confirmed re-entry floor — the level at which a modest cushion would be restored — sits meaningfully below the current close, roughly 1.8% lower and just under the nervous-zone approach. That floor, not the fib ladder, is the level at which valuation would begin to cooperate with a technical re-entry.

Two things could change this. Either the 10-year eases, restoring cushion without price having to fall, or earnings revisions push forward estimates higher. Neither has happened. A further rise in yields from here would push the premium negative, which under the posture guide would shift the stance from "trim bias" to "trim only." The three-year and six-month price records make the same point visually: the index has re-rated aggressively since April while the discount rate has not cooperated, and the resulting compression is why the June–July advance has repeatedly stalled at the same ceiling.
