## Market Regime

The structural regime remains **Late Bull / Topping** — a maturing but intact uptrend carrying clear late-cycle warning signs. Each of the four regime tests points to a healthy price skeleton shadowed by weakening internal support.

- **Price extension:** SPX sits +7.83% above its rising 200-day average — moderate-to-elevated extension. Not extreme, but enough to blunt full drift benefit and keep mean-reversion pressure live near resistance.
- **Valuation support (ERP):** The equity risk premium is a razor-thin 0.37% and stable, sitting squarely in the 0.0–0.5% valuation-ceiling band. After the 10-year rose to 4.61%, equities offer virtually no cushion over the risk-free rate — a structural headwind, not a support.
- **Credit confirmation:** The junk bond yield spread widened back to 1.30, right at its recent average. Credit is neither escalating alarm nor confirming equity strength — it declines to validate the price recovery.
- **Breadth confirmation:** This is the weakest leg. The McClellan Volume Summation Index sits near its lows around 948, and net new 52-week highs remain feeble at roughly 1.95. Participation is narrow; the recovery has been carried by leadership rather than broad advance.

Put together, a rising trend structure (50-day above a rising 200-day, price above both intermediate averages, benign volatility) is colliding with a zero-cushion valuation, unconfirming credit, and deteriorating breadth. That is the textbook profile of a late-cycle market — one where price can still grind higher but where the odds of a clean, broadly-supported trend extension are lower than the price chart alone suggests. The regime classification, held for six sessions running, remains appropriate and calls for a higher bar on any bullish action.
