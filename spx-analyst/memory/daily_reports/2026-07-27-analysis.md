## Today's Posture

Defense and patience. Hold existing trims, deploy no new capital, and let the market resolve a fourth consecutive session of confirmation failure before committing anything.

The index closed essentially unchanged at 7,413, its third straight day of basing beneath the 20-day and 50-day moving-average cluster and below the entire 23.6%–61.8% retracement band of the July 8–15 leg. That is not a collapse — the intraday shape was constructive, with a morning flush absorbed and a close in the upper third of the range — but it is a stall in exactly the place where a healthy bull would be reclaiming lost ground. Four sessions of failing to get back over the 50-day is information.

Nothing in the confirming layers has improved enough to change that. McClellan breadth has slid to its cycle-low reference, net new 52-week highs continue to fade, the equity risk premium remains pinned inside the valuation-ceiling band, and volatility sits above its 50-day average without ever producing the capitulation spike that marks a tradeable washout. The probability model is decisively down-first and fails every regime threshold outright.

Signal alignment is four trim conditions against one buy condition. The posture is unambiguous: protect capital, keep dry powder, and require either a genuine reclaim of the 50-day with breadth participation, or a proper flush with volatility confirmation, before re-engaging.

## Market Regime

The structural bias remains Late Bull / Topping, unchanged for a seventh consecutive session, and every input into that classification points the same direction.

On extension, price sits roughly 5.8% above the 200-day moving average — moderate extension, not extreme. This is the single most bullish structural input available today, because it means the index is not stretched in the way that precedes violent mean reversion, and it justifies keeping full drift in the probability model with a Low Rally Exhaustion Score. The three-year view supports this: an unbroken sequence of higher highs and higher lows since the 2023 base, with the 200-day rising steadily beneath price.

On valuation support, the equity risk premium is stable but sits inside the band where equities offer essentially no premium over risk-free yield. Structural support is neither improving nor deteriorating — it is simply absent as a cushion.

Credit is the one genuinely neutral input. The junk-versus-investment-grade spread sits right at its one-year average and has eased from the July spike, so credit is not flashing a warning and is not confirming a risk-off break either.

Breadth is where the regime call is decided. The McClellan Volume Summation Index has fallen from roughly 1,290 in May to 834 today, sitting directly on its cycle-low reference line, while net new 52-week highs have decayed from nearly 3.9 in May to 1.54. Participation has contracted for three months while the index held near its highs. Narrow leadership plus a zero valuation cushion plus flat credit is the textbook late-cycle signature, and it sets the higher 70% action bar for any bullish deployment.

- The close landed in the upper third of the daily range after a morning flush to 7,388 was steadily bought back into a late-afternoon push to 7,422.

That upper-third close is the day's genuine positive and deserves acknowledgment — sellers did not control the tape into the bell. But a strong close inside a range that remains below both the 20-day and the 50-day is stabilization, not repair. The three-month and six-month charts show the index has spent all of July grinding in a distribution-shaped range beneath a flattening 50-day, with each rally attempt into the swing-high ceiling rejected. Until the 50-day is reclaimed and held, the working read is a maturing trend that has lost its short-term momentum.

## Valuation and ERP

Valuation is the reason no dip here qualifies as a buy, even after a 2.2% pullback.

The forward multiple sits in the band that would normally support moderate buying at support, and that is worth noting as a mitigating factor — the market is not trading at a bubble multiple. The problem is the comparison against bonds. With the 10-year easing modestly, the equity risk premium ticked up but remains inside the 0.0%–0.5% valuation-ceiling zone, where equities deliver essentially no compensation for taking equity risk over the risk-free alternative. The trend is stable, meaning this is a persistent condition rather than a one-day artifact — the cushion has been absent for well over a week now.

The practical consequence is the ERP-confirmed re-entry floor. Price must fall to that level before valuation alone begins supporting adds, and today that floor sits only a fraction of a percent below spot. That proximity is a double-edged observation: a modest further decline would restore a minimal cushion, but "minimal cushion at 0.5%" is the bottom rung of the reduced-aggression band, not a green light. Restoring genuine valuation support would require either a materially deeper price correction, a meaningful decline in yields, or upward earnings revisions.

The posture that follows is trim bias with no aggressive adds. A moderate forward multiple keeps this from being a trim-only situation, but with no premium over Treasuries, capital has no valuation reason to be deployed into a market whose technical structure is simultaneously broken.

Five tensions remain live today, and the validated posture resolves each toward defense.

**Breadth versus the index-level trend.** The bullish reading is that price is only about 2.2% off the swing high with the 50-day still above a rising 200-day, so the uptrend has not broken and laggards can catch up. The bearish reading is that the summation index has fallen to its cycle-low reference from the May peak while net new highs decayed from nearly 3.9 to 1.54 — three months of contracting participation beneath a flat index. Resolution: this is the highest-weight conflict and it dominates. Narrow leadership at cycle-low breadth is precisely why the Late Bull classification holds and why no rally attempt qualifies as confirmed.

**Down-first probabilities versus the intact bull skeleton.** Bullishly, exhaustion is Low, drift is full, and the projected path grinds back toward the SMA cluster and beyond over 30 to 60 days. Bearishly, first-hit odds are roughly two-to-one down-first with a five-day median to the caution level versus fifteen days to the swing high, failing all three thresholds. Resolution: the model is read through the structural bias, not in isolation — a favorable medium-term drift path does not license deployment when near-term ordering is adverse and no threshold is met.

**Valuation ceiling versus a moderate multiple.** Bullishly, the forward multiple sits in the moderate-buying band and easing yields nudged the risk premium higher. Bearishly, the premium remains inside the ceiling band with the re-entry floor still below spot. Resolution: trim bias with no aggressive adds; the multiple prevents a trim-only stance but provides no reason to buy.

**Sentiment reset without capitulation.** Bullishly, fear-zone sentiment and put/call at the top of its range are contrarian early-bottoming conditions. Bearishly, volatility never spiked toward 25–30, so the decline is orderly rather than washed out. Resolution: a watch condition, not a trigger — patience until volatility confirms.

**Short-term money flow versus long-term momentum.** Bullishly, MFI has lifted from the high-30s to the mid-50s while price flatlined. Bearishly, daily RSI has traced lower highs from 78 in June to the mid-40s across three months. Resolution: the longer divergence outweighs the shorter one; the MFI uptick explains the three-day base but does not repair the trend.
